Create a polished, stylized low-poly 3D model of a homeless man holding a small paper cup and carrying a worn backpack using Three.js. Deliver the entire project as a single `index.html` file containing all HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Use a CDN for Three.js and OrbitControls. Generate all geometry procedurally: no external models, textures, images, or additional local files.

VISUAL STYLE

Use an intentional low-poly art style with visible flat-shaded surfaces, clean silhouettes, and a harmonious, muted color palette. The result should look like a carefully designed game character with recognizable anatomy, layered clothing, and personal belongings. Avoid making the character look like a stack of unrelated primitives.

Use slightly exaggerated proportions: a relatively large head, a sturdy but slightly slouched torso, expressive facial features, large hands, and substantial worn shoes. Give the character personality through his posture, face, and clothing.

Aim for the visual quality of a memorable supporting character in a stylized adventure game. His expression should feel tired, calm, and thoughtful.

CHARACTER

Create a full-body adult homeless man, approximately 45–55 years old, standing in a relaxed, slightly hunched pose on a small urban sidewalk.

Head and face:
- An angular head with a warm, slightly weathered skin tone, defined cheekbones, cheeks, and jaw.
- A noticeable but proportionate protruding nose.
- Two clearly visible eyes with eyelids, small pupils, and subtle bags underneath.
- Thick, slightly uneven eyebrows.
- A closed mouth with a neutral, mildly weary expression.
- An untrimmed gray-brown beard built from several connected angular masses following the cheeks, jaw, chin, and upper lip.
- Keep the beard shaped around the face rather than attaching a single cone beneath the head.
- Small ears positioned correctly on both sides.
- Uneven dark brown hair with gray streaks, visible around the temples and beneath the hat.
- A faded burgundy knitted beanie with a folded rim, a slightly slouched crown, and a few broad geometric ribs suggesting knitted fabric.
- Keep the eyes, nose, and expression readable beneath the beanie.

Clothing:
- A faded brown oversized jacket worn open over a muted mustard sweater.
- Give the jacket visible thickness, separate front panels, a collar, broad angular folds, and a slightly uneven hem.
- Include two large pockets, simple buttons, elbow patches, and a few small worn areas.
- Represent patches and damage with carefully placed geometry and solid-color materials.
- Avoid random surface noise or excessive tiny details.
- Add a faded blue-gray scarf loosely wrapped around the neck, with one short end hanging over the chest.
- Make the scarf sit naturally between the neck and jacket without obscuring the entire face.
- Dark charcoal trousers with distinct hips, thighs, slightly bent knees, and lower legs.
- Add a cloth patch on one knee, a small tear on the other, and uneven trouser cuffs.
- Worn dark brown work boots with thick soles, defined toes, simplified laces, and contrasting scuffed areas.
- Make one boot slightly more worn than the other while keeping their construction consistent.
- Fingerless gloves in faded dark green, leaving the thumbs and fingertips visible.

Pose and anatomy:
- Position both feet securely on the pavement, approximately shoulder-width apart, with one foot slightly forward and turned outward.
- Shift most of the weight onto the rear leg while maintaining believable balance.
- Give the knees a slight bend and the shoulders a gentle forward slump.
- Tilt the head slightly and turn it toward the hand holding the cup.
- Build arms with clear shoulders, upper arms, elbows, forearms, wrists, and hands.
- One arm should extend gently forward at waist height, holding a paper cup upright.
- The other arm should bend toward the chest, with the hand gripping a backpack shoulder strap.
- Construct simplified fingers and thumbs that visibly wrap around the cup and strap.
- Ensure the shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, and knees connect naturally without gaps or obvious intersections.
- Use overlapping clothing layers and shaped geometry to create coherent transitions between body parts.

PAPER CUP

Make the cup small but clearly recognizable:
- A slightly tapered paper cup in a faded cream color.
- A visible rolled rim, thin walls, and a recessed interior.
- A muted red horizontal band made from geometry.
- A few small coins visible inside when viewed from above.
- Keep the cup upright and positioned directly inside the gripping hand.
- Place the fingers around its lower half and the thumb against the opposite side.
- Do not let the cup float beside the hand or intersect the palm unnaturally.

BACKPACK AND BELONGINGS

Create a worn backpack that feels like a meaningful part of the character:
- A faded olive canvas backpack with a slightly sagging, irregular silhouette.
- A shaped main compartment, a thick top flap, and a smaller front pocket.
- Two broad shoulder straps that follow the shoulders and connect visibly to the bag.
- Visible strap thickness, simple buckles, and a short carrying handle.
- A few contrasting fabric patches and simplified seam lines.
- A rolled sleeping blanket secured horizontally beneath the backpack with two straps.
- Use a muted blue-gray color for the blanket, with a visible spiral or layered fold at each end.
- Make the blanket slightly uneven rather than a perfect cylinder.
- Attach a small metal mug to the side of the backpack with a visible loop.
- Keep all attachments physically connected and proportional to the character.

The hand gripping the shoulder strap must wrap around it convincingly. The strap should remain continuous above and below the hand.

Use a subtle neutral background that complements the muted clothing.

CONTROLS AND ANIMATION

Add OrbitControls so the viewer can rotate around the model, zoom in, and inspect it from all sides. Set a sensible orbit target, zoom limits, and polar-angle limits to prevent the camera from going beneath the pavement.

Include subtle idle animation:
- Gentle breathing in the chest and shoulders.
- A very slight movement of the head.
- Minimal coordinated movement of the arms with the torso.
- A restrained secondary sway in the hanging scarf end and attached metal mug.

Keep the feet firmly planted. Do not animate the entire character up and down.

Keep the cup attached to its hand and approximately upright throughout the animation. Keep the other hand connected to the backpack strap. The backpack and straps should follow the torso naturally.
