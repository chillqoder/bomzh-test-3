Create a polished, stylized low-poly 3D diorama of a small landfill using Three.js. Deliver the entire project as a single `index.html` file containing all HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Use a CDN for Three.js and OrbitControls. Generate all geometry procedurally: no external models, textures, images, or additional local files.

The result should look like a carefully composed miniature environment from a stylized game: a compact island of dirt covered with recognizable discarded objects, organized trash piles, a muddy access road, and a small weathered bulldozer.

This is an interactive display scene, not a game. The viewer should be able to rotate around the diorama, zoom in, and inspect its details.

VISUAL STYLE

Use an intentional low-poly art style with visible flat-shaded surfaces, angular shapes, clean silhouettes, and a harmonious color palette.

Make the landfill feel cluttered and neglected while keeping its composition readable. Avoid covering the ground with uniformly scattered random primitives.

Use muted earthy colors:
- Dusty brown soil.
- Gray-green mud.
- Faded olive and charcoal garbage bags.
- Rust-red metal.
- Dirty cream appliances.
- Weathered yellow machinery.
- Small accents of faded blue, green, and orange plastic.

Create wear through geometry, missing panels, dents, tilted objects, and restrained color variations. Do not use photorealistic textures.

DIORAMA COMPOSITION

Build a compact, irregular display base approximately 22 units wide and 18 units deep.

Arrange the scene into three visual layers:
- Foreground: a muddy entrance, shallow puddle, scattered bottles, and a few tires.
- Middle ground: the main trash mound, a tipped dumpster, and a small bulldozer.
- Background: lower secondary trash piles, a damaged fence, and sparse dry vegetation.

The main trash mound should be the dominant feature. The bulldozer should provide a secondary focal point and a clear sense of scale.

Leave some exposed ground and a readable access path between objects. Keep the entire diorama visible from the initial camera position.

GROUND AND BASE

Create an irregular patch of earth with a clearly visible edge and thickness:
- An uneven, triangulated top surface.
- Low hills and shallow depressions.
- Exposed soil layers along the sides.
- A darker lower layer and a lighter dusty surface.
- A few embedded stones near the edges.

Make the surface and side walls connect without cracks.

Add a curved muddy vehicle path entering from the foreground and continuing toward the main mound.

Suggest wheel ruts using shallow geometric grooves or darker inset strips that follow the terrain. Avoid flat decorative lines floating above the ground.

Place a few angular rocks, sparse weeds, and short dry grass clusters around the perimeter.

MAIN TRASH MOUND

Create one large asymmetrical mound and two smaller supporting piles.

Build each pile with:
- An irregular underlying mass that provides volume.
- Overlapping recognizable objects partially embedded in the surface.
- Larger discarded objects near the bottom.
- Smaller debris filling gaps.
- An uneven silhouette with several distinct peaks and depressions.

Do not make the mound look like a smooth cone, a pyramid, or a heap of identical cubes.

Use controlled, seeded variation for object placement, rotation, scale, and color. Keep objects supported by the pile or by neighboring objects. Avoid floating debris and implausibly balanced arrangements.

RECOGNIZABLE TRASH

Include a carefully balanced selection of the following objects:

Garbage bags:
- Irregular bulging bodies with angular surfaces.
- Narrow tied necks and small twisted tops.
- Several different sizes.
- Mostly charcoal, dirty olive, and faded blue.

Cardboard boxes:
- Open and closed variations.
- Visible wall thickness.
- Bent flaps and slightly crushed corners.
- Muted brown colors with darker fold lines.

Glass bottles:
- Recognizable bodies, shoulders, narrow necks, and lips.
- Green, brown, and pale blue variations.
- Some upright, others lying on their sides.
- Keep most bottles opaque or only lightly transparent for clarity and performance.

Metal cans:
- Short cylinders with defined rims.
- A few crushed versions with irregular profiles.
- Restrained metallic highlights.

Old tires:
- Thick dark rubber rings with visible center openings.
- A few simple geometric tread segments.
- Some stacked, others leaning or half-buried.
- Avoid perfectly smooth black doughnuts.

Scrap metal:
- Bent sheet-metal panels with visible thickness.
- Rusted pipes and a few short beams.
- An overturned shopping basket or simplified metal frame.

Keep small debris secondary. Use fewer well-shaped objects rather than thousands of tiny particles.

Make shadows ground the bulldozer, dumpster, appliances, and trash piles without turning the gaps into unreadable black areas.

Use a subtle warm-gray background and a large matte presentation surface beneath the diorama to receive soft shadows.

Choose an elevated three-quarter initial camera view that clearly shows:
- The full irregular base.
- The access path and puddle.
- The main trash mound.
- The refrigerator and sofa.
- The tipped dumpster.
- The bulldozer and its front blade.

Keep comfortable margins around the entire scene.

CONTROLS AND ANIMATION

Add OrbitControls with:
- Smooth damping.
- A target near the visual center of the diorama.
- Sensible minimum and maximum zoom distances.
- Polar-angle limits that prevent the camera from passing beneath the base.

Include restrained ambient animation:
- A few dry grass clusters moving slightly in the breeze.
- One loose piece of paper gently lifting and settling near the ground.
- Occasional subtle expanding ripples in the puddle.

Keep the trash piles, machinery, and large props stationary. Avoid constant motion, exaggerated effects, smoke clouds, or particle storms.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

- Everything must be contained in one `index.html`.
- Use ES modules and a CDN import map.
- Pin Three.js and OrbitControls to exactly the same version.
- Use procedural geometry and solid-color materials.
- Use flat shading where appropriate.
- Use custom low-poly geometry where basic primitives would weaken the silhouette.
- Organize the ground, trash piles, props, vehicle, fence, and animated details into logical groups.
- Reuse geometries and materials.
- Use instancing where useful for repeated bottles, cans, fence elements, and track plates.
- Use seeded randomness so the composition remains consistent after reloading.
- Avoid z-fighting, disconnected geometry, and excessive transparent surfaces.
- Make the canvas responsive to window resizing.
- Support high-DPI screens with a pixel-ratio limit of 2.
- Use frame-rate-independent animation.
- Render smoothly in a modern browser.
- Avoid interface panels, labels, buttons, and text overlays.
- Allow the 3D scene to occupy the screen.
